You asked me in your journal · Thu Oct 29

Eight tostadas, by the numbers

"Sage, if you could let me know what you think that is, a guesstimate." Here it is, plus where your other notes to me landed.

The short answer

About 1,700 calories, give or take a tostada

Your recipe: corn tostadas about 5 inches across, refried beans, ground beef, a little cilantro and onion, and Green Iguana on top. Not piled high, but thick. Per tostada, roughly:

The shell, fried corn at 5 inches: 50 to 60. The beans, a thick smear of 3 to 4 tablespoons: 40 to 70. The beef, an ounce to an ounce and a half cooked: 75 to 115. Onion, cilantro, hot sauce: under 10.

That's about 170 to 250 a tostada, so eight lands between roughly 1,400 and 2,000. Call it 1,700. There's also something like 90 to 120 grams of protein in there, mostly the beef. If the beans were made with lard or the beef was a fattier grind, lean toward the top of the range. It's a guesstimate from typical label values, not a weigh-in; one plate on a kitchen scale would turn it into a real number.

For scale, that's roughly four of Monday's walks up Zipline Mountain. Green Iguana is zero calories, so technically the sauce was the healthy part.

You asked me in your journal · Tue Oct 20

Was Merlin a Christian?

"If you want to look that up and give me some more depth of feel, I'm feeling very called to go into my understanding of Merlin more." Here it is.

The short answer

The Christian Merlin came about sixty years after the first one

The first Merlin. Geoffrey of Monmouth brought Merlin into the world around 1136, in his History of the Kings of Britain: a prophet-boy fathered by a spirit on a mortal mother, full of visions and with very little church in him.

The Christian Merlin. Around 1200 a French poet, Robert de Boron, rewrote him for the Grail story. In Robert's version the demons plot to father an Antichrist. His mother's goodness, and a quick baptism arranged by her confessor Blaise, spoil the plan. So Merlin keeps his father's gift, knowing everything that has happened, and God adds his own, knowing what is to come. He chooses to put both in God's service, and he's the one who has the Round Table built to seat the Grail knights. That's the Christian throughline you noticed, and most retellings since borrow from it.

Thoth and Hermes. That link is old and documented: in Hellenistic Egypt the Greeks merged their Hermes with Egypt's Thoth into Hermes Trismegistus, "thrice great." Merlin joining that line is a modern esoteric reading rather than an ancient one, which makes it yours to feel. You said it feels right in your system.

A wizard born to be the Antichrist who chose to serve the light anyway. No wonder you couldn't stop watching.
